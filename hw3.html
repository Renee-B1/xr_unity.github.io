A description of the environment you created and the triggers you placed in it. But more importantly, describe the story you are trying to tell and how the player experiences it as they walk through your space:

I created a forest-savanna mosaic environment featuring some animals you would find in their natural habitat. Players are meant to approach the environment like it is a safari, discovering and interacting with the diverse characteristics of the areas and animals. The story is what you make it and how you observe the space; a herd of elephants visiting a rushing waterfall for a quick drink, lions roaring from atop jagged rocks warning any who come close, or monkeys deep in the forest fighting back against an unchecked power.

Three key takeaways about designing walkable, legible 3D space (environment, trigger placement, player guidance):

1. Having the environment be open rather than enclosed is both a strength and weakness of this build in terms of walkability. With it being open I did not have to worry much about things being too close to one another or sharing relative space. This meant I wouldn't have to find a way to help the user navigate around things as much and could add as much as I wanted to grow the narrative. Though with an open space that also means the user can get lost, can find the worlds bounds, and may even become uninterested in exploring such a vast plain.

2. Learning how to make triggers and manipulating trigger elements helped a lot with interactivity. It helped in telling the open world story by resizing the trigger area so the player did not have to walk directly on top of something to activate its event.

3. Colliders are, and forever will be, our best friend.

What was the hardest part of this assignment? What did you learn about Unity, XRIT, or your own design process that will help you in future assignments?

Hardest part of the assignment was probably organizing (I use this word loosely) and modifying each added element. I added a lot of poly models and needed to grapple with where some files were or where they were placed when putting them into the project. I also had to color blades of grass and trees by hand because their textures did not load with their download (I don't want to talk about it). What I learned about Unity and my own design process is that like a lot of software I have used, if something does not work or isn't going the way you planned it, there are ways to get around any issue. Like for example, point/ spot lights were not working for illuminating the sun the way I wanted so Dr. Wang suggested adding emission to the colors instead to simulate the same effect.
